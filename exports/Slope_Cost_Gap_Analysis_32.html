Slope Score v11 — model assessment + competitive comparison
Validated March 10, 2026 · Model Risk Rating: HIGH · OOF AUC: 0.748 ± 0.038 · 963,847 training rows · 497 observed outcomes · Overall grade: B−
Model strengths
+
Strong stability improvement over v10
Daily score volatility 3.29 → 0.94 (3.4× better). Tier change rate 14.0% → 4.4%. Score range expanded 24–81 → 8.5–100. Policy backtest: +23% gross profit, approval 43.7% vs 31.3%.
+
Slope TransFormer — market-unique LLM upstream
Patent-pending transaction categorizer trained on bank language. Surpasses Plaid, ChatGPT, and humans on SMB categorization. No competitor has an equivalent.
+
Strong charge-off segmentation + CECL stress framework
Tier 1 (0.5%) vs Tier 5 (10.3%) — 20× spread. Has CECL-aligned macro stress testing across 3 scenarios. Monotonic constraints + stability-penalized objective is sophisticated for stage.
+
Validated: 0 Findings, 5 Recommendations, 4 Observations
Approved March 10, 2026. Documentation grade A — exceeds fintech norms, approaching bank standards. Team understands they're building toward institutional quality.
Model limitations
−
Pro-cyclical under stress — collapses when it matters most
CECL stress testing exists, but results are alarming: approval rate 48.9% baseline → 6.6% under mild recession (−1% to −2% GDP) → 0.0% under CCAR severe. Score std dev compresses 23.2 → 6.3 — loses discriminatory power exactly when SMBs need credit.
−
497 observed outcomes — severely limiting
Major banks require 10,000–100,000+. Fintech peers typically 2,000–20,000. 81% of training is reject inference. Per-fold AUC ranges 0.695–0.781 (high variance). Wide confidence intervals limit pricing precision.
−
Plaid single-point-of-failure — no fallback model
All 12 features derive from Plaid transaction data. 2 features have 34% null rates in training. If Plaid connection fails, model cannot score. No bureau-only fallback scorecard exists.
−
Internal-only validation — violates SR 11-7
Validator Ron Liu is on the same DS team as developer Jason Huang. HIGH risk model. Would be flagged by OCC/Fed examiners. Bank partnership requires external independent validation.
